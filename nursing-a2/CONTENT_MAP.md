p4 burden: No.1 killer globally, ~1/3 deaths, Australia prevalence 18%, mortality 27% (Heart Foundation)
p5 anatomy: chambers, valves, vessels, septum, conduction system, pericardium (diagram: tricuspid R, mitral L, pulmonary, aortic valves)
p6 CV assessment: medical history, risk factors, physical exam, diagnostic tests
p7 physical exam: inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation
p8 inspection images: tongue/hands (central cyanosis?), fingers — captions none (don't interpret)
p9 inspection images: neck (JVD?) and foot pitting oedema — no captions
p10 palpation pulse strength: 3+ full bounding; 2+ normal/strong; 1+ weak diminished thready; 0 absent. Pulse sites: carotid, brachial, radial, ulnar, femoral, popliteal, posterior tibial, dorsalis pedis
p11 resting HR chart (agelessinvesting.com) — skip values
p12 palpate PMI; note thrills or heaves
p13 percussion image
p14 auscultation: listening for a bruit; listening to heart sounds (image: stethoscope carotid)
p15 S1 = closure of mitral & tricuspid (start systole); S2 = closure aortic & pulmonary (A2 P2) (start diastole)
p16 auscultation areas: aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, apical, epigastric
p17 heart sounds vs ECG: AV valves close = S1; semilunar close = S2; S3, S4 shown; ventricular systole/diastole
p18 murmurs: heart valve disease; valve stenosis images
p19 BP categories (AHA): normal <120 and <80; elevated 120-129 and <80; stage1 130-139 or 80-89; stage2 >=140 or >=90; crisis >180 and/or >120 (consult doctor immediately)
p20 blood tests: lipid profile, fasting BGL, cardiac biomarkers (troponin, CK, CK-MB)
p21 diagnostics: ECG, echo, exercise stress test, coronary angiography, Holter, cardiac CT/MRI
p22 conduction: SA node, internodal tracts, Bachmann's bundle, AV node, bundle branches L/R, conduction pathways
p23 cardiac monitoring photo (3 electrodes)
p24 ECG image: chest electrodes, ankle electrode, machine, trace (12-lead picture) — no positions stated
p25 2nd-year should identify: NSR; life-threatening arrhythmias VT, VF, asystole
p26 normal ECG PQRST
p27 P wave = activation of atria; QRS = activation of ventricles; T = recovery wave; strips normal/fast/slow/irregular
p28 "What is this rhythm?" strip (regular, P before QRS) — answer not given on slide
p29 atrial fibrillation strip
p30 VT: rate 100-250, regular, P waves usually not identifiable, PR not measured, QRS >=0.12 s bizarre. Treat if sustained, symptomatic, or organic heart disease; DC cardioversion or IV procainamide, lignocaine, class III antiarrhythmic if haemodynamic instability; ablation or ICD for repeated episodes
p31 VF: no real rate, grossly irregular, no identifiable P waves, PR none, QRS bizarre varying; immediate cardioversion/defibrillation
p32 ACS = unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI

p35 after discharge: cardiac rehab, adherence to meds, lifestyle changes, regular check-ups

Give salbutamol via CONTINUOUS nebulisation; use oxygen to drive nebuliser (*piped oxygen or cylinder with high-flow regulator (6 L/min)); children 1-5 & 6-11 maintain SaO2 >=95%; adults: titrate oxygen to target SaO2 93-95% in adults, >=95% in adolescents. Life-threatening: any of drowsy, collapsed, exhausted, cyanotic, poor respiratory effort, SpO2 <90%. Severe: start oxygen, titrate to target sat at least 95% (children). WITHIN MINUTES reassess severity; continue bronchodilator; if poor response add IV MgSO4; WITHIN FIRST HOUR start systemic corticosteroids (oral prednisolone ...); 1 HOUR reassess response (spirometry if capable, repeat pulse oximetry). # STROKE.pdf ("Cerebrovascular Anatomy and Stroke Management", Siobhan Hawdon Neurosurgery CNC; credit Rochelle Firth NP) — subject not stated. p = PDF page
p3 brain functions: frontal (movement, reasoning, behaviour, memory, decision making, personality, planning, judgement, initiative, inhibition, mood); parietal (right from left, calculations, sensations, reading, writing); occipital (vision); temporal (language comprehension, behaviour, memory, hearing, emotions); cerebellum (balance, coordination, fine muscle control); brain stem (breathing, BP, heartbeat, swallowing); pituitary (hormones, growth, fertility). Identifying the area affected lets us recognise symptoms & give patient-centred care
p4 left hemisphere: sensory from RIGHT side, motor control RIGHT side, speech/language/comprehension, analysis & calculations, time & sequencing, recognition of words/letters/numbers. Right hemisphere: sensory LEFT, motor LEFT, creativity, spatial ability, context/perception, recognition of faces/places/objects
p5 vascular supply: circle of Willis, internal carotid, basilar, vertebral; ACA, MCA, PCA highlighted
p6 territories: ACA / MCA / PCA colour map (MCA lateral)
p8 homunculus with ACA (leg/foot/hip), MCA (face, arm, hand), PCA
p9 stroke = disruption of blood flow to brain, blocked or reduced; ischaemic, haemorrhagic; early recognition & treatment vital; up to 1.9 million brain cells per minute; reduced blood flow -> symptoms -> reversible (ischaemia) or permanent (infarct)
p10 ischaemic: 4 out of 5 strokes; embolic = clot forms and travels to brain; thrombotic = plaque formation; infarct core & ischaemic penumbra; TIA (mini stroke) temporary blockage; 1 in 5 (TIA) will have major stroke within next 3 months
p11 haemorrhagic: acute manifestation of underlying small vessel disease; rupture of small vessels -> haematoma expands over 4-24 h -> pressure -> vessel rupture -> compression of neuronal tissue (30-90 min arrow); 30% of ICH increase in volume; causes HTN, vessel abnormality, malignancy, trauma; 1 in 5 strokes haemorrhagic; mortality ~40% at 1 month, 54% at 1 year; functional independence 20%
p12 SAH: 85% ruptured aneurysm; 30-40% death; 20-35% severe injury/disability; 15-30% mild deficits; require vascular treatment due to risk of re-rupture
p13 aneurysm = weakening in artery wall, ballooning/bulging; saccular, fusiform, ruptured; unruptured may cause symptoms if very large; ruptured -> SAH, medical emergency; RF smoking, HTN, family history, age >40, women > men
p14 surgical: craniotomy & clipping; clip in situ permanently; aneurysm thromboses & collapses
p15 endovascular: stenting & coiling via DSA (angiogram), less invasive; metal coils embolise; stents sometimes; requires dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT)
p16 Australian stats (Stroke Foundation): 2023 ~45,785 strokes (1 every 11 min); 5th leading cause of death; regional 17% more likely; more deaths than breast & prostate cancer; 80% preventable; 2023 1 in 4 strokes <65; ~30% survivors <65 (working age); top 10 causes of death 45+
p17 age-related changes: cerebral amyloidosis (70s), atherosclerosis, decreased baroreceptor function, wear & tear, neuronal loss, reduced new neuronal growth (reduced recovery), decreased cerebral blood flow & metabolic rate (>25% by 80s)
p18 risk factors hexagons: labelled "Non-modifiable" row: older age, gender, family history, vascular abnormalities, atrial fibrillation, diabetes; "Modifiable": high cholesterol, smoking, oral anticoagulants, excessive alcohol, obesity & inactivity, high BP [AF/diabetes placement ambiguous — avoid]
p19 F.A.S.T.: Face drooped? Arms can't be raised? Speech slurred or confused? Time is critical! Call 000. "If you see any of these signs act FAST call triple zero (000)"
p20 initial assessment: symptom onset, PMHx, medications; physical & neuro assessment: vital signs, GCS + NIHSS. Diagnosis: CT (non-contrast), CTA/CT perfusion, MRI, DSA -> treatment
p21 GCS: check (factors interfering with communication, ability to respond, other injuries), observe (eye opening, content of speech, movements of right & left sides), stimulate (sound: spoken or shouted request; physical: pressure on fingertip, trapezius or supraorbital notch), rate (assign according to HIGHEST response observed). Chart: eyes 4 spontaneously, 3 to speech, 2 to pain, 1 none; verbal 5 orientated, 4 confused, 3 inappropriate words, 2 incomprehensible sounds, 1 none; motor 6 obeys commands, 5 localises to pain, 4 withdraws, 3 flexion to pain, 2 extension to pain, 1 none. Clinical review if GCS drops by 1 point; rapid response if GCS drops by 2 or more points. Limb strength 5 normal ... 0 no movement. Pupils size/reaction
p22 NIHSS: 11 domains, score 0-42; used with GCS; guides treatment & prognosis
p23 medical mgmt: identify type (haemorrhagic vs ischaemic) -> prevent worsening cellular damage -> treat primary injury (reperfusion, surgical mgmt, reversal of anticoagulation) + prevent secondary brain injury (hypertension, hyperglycaemia, infection, hypoxia). Ischaemic: thrombolysis <4.5 h; endovascular clot retrieval <24 h; carotid endarterectomy; consider antiplatelets, anticoagulants, antihypertensive, statins. Haemorrhagic: ventricular drain; craniotomy & repair; endovascular repair; consider antihypertensive
p24 ECR: return blood flow, often with thrombolysis; catheter via femoral or radial artery; wire stent or suction device; up to 24 h (ideally <6 h) from onset for some patients after scan
p25 thrombolysis tPA: IV tenecteplase or alteplase, clot dissolving, targets fibrin; as early as possible, up to 4.5 h after onset; stroke team care; post-thrombolysis neuro (NIHSS) assessment & vital sign monitoring. Contraindicated: haemorrhagic stroke; large ischaemic area on imaging (>1/3); active bleeding, recent trauma or surgery; coagulopathy (low platelets, high INR); anticoagulants within 48 h
p26 ACI guide: triage category 2, neuro assessment & confirm time of onset -> direct to CT; noncontrast CT rules out ICH; 1 in 3 thrombolysed patients improved outcomes; appropriate patient selection
p27 telestroke; early rehab (assessment <48 h); multidisciplinary; stroke units improve outcomes; "All stroke patients should be admitted to hospital and treated in a stroke unit with an interdisciplinary team" (strong recommendation)
p28 nursing considerations: neuro monitoring; BP mgmt (avoid hyper/hypotension); dysphagia (swallow assessment); fevers & infection; DVT prophylaxis; hyperglycaemia; oral care; pressure area care & positioning; communication deficits (dysphasia); nutrition; fatigue/depression; engage family & patients; falls risk; delirium; medication mgmt
p29 time is brain; every second counts
