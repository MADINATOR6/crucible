# CONFLICTS

Where loaded sources disagree or are easy to confuse. The app shows both sides, labelled, and no question depends on picking one side.

## Between 93225 sources

| # | Point | Source A | Source B | How the app handles it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C5 | Nasal prong flow | 93225 Canvas page 2.2: 1-4 L; above 4 L needs humidification, which standard prongs can't provide | Section 12.5 (p.21): 24-44% at 1-6 L/min; humidification may be recommended above 4 L/min; FiO2 doesn't rise above 6 L/min | Oxygen notes and study guide show both. For the lab, the Canvas figure is what your tutors wrote |
| C6 | Hudson (simple face) mask flow | Canvas page 2.2: 5-10 L | Section 12.5 (p.24): 40-60% at 5-8 L/min; at least 5 L/min to prevent CO2 rebreathing | Both shown. Agreed: at least 5 L/min |
| C7 | General SpO2 target | TSANZ 2022 (p.2): 92-96% (give O2 when SpO2 < 92%) | Section 12.5 (p.14, p.36): 94-98%. Lecture 3 / PACSA (p.12, p.27): O2 not needed in stable ACS if SpO2 ≥ 93%, titrate to > 93% | TSANZ is the Module 2 assigned reading and is used in the guide; the others are labelled. All agree on 88-92% for hypercapnia risk |
| C8 | Nebuliser driving gas | TSANZ (p.2): in COPD, air-driven, with O2 continued via nasal cannulae. Lab 2 workbook: 'Apply the prescribed air flow' | Section 12.6 (p.16): hospitals often use oxygen instead of the compressor, with SpO2 monitored. FNP2B asthma Handbook figure: oxygen-driven for life-threatening asthma | Context-dependent (COPD vs acute asthma). Both shown in the nebuliser notes and guide |
| C9 | Nebuliser flow | Lecture 2 (slide 16): 6-8 L/min; above 10 L/min is too fast | FNP2B asthma Handbook figure: high-flow regulator 6 L/min | Compatible; Lecture 2 is the 93225 instruction |
| C10 | Medication rights | Section 12.6 (p.13-16): six rights | Lab 2 workbook (p.~7): '5 rights 3 times' | Both kept in the two nebuliser checklist versions |
